A / CONSTRUCTION NOTES

Color ratio

One contiguous violet field occupies one third of the canvas. The entire five-stage register remains neutral and readable.

Typography

Violet-side brand statement: 40–55px. Stage titles: 18–24px. Description text: 13px. No oversized phase numerals.

Reflow

On narrow screens, the violet brand field becomes a short opening block. The five stages follow vertically with intact reading order.

B / OPEN EDITORIAL

Brand through type.
Space through editorial judgment.

No colored block or large pictogram. Instead, the color participates in the typographic hierarchy: one emphatic violet headline, precisely aligned stage names, and clear compositional air.

B / CONSTRUCTION NOTES

Color role

Violet appears in the major typographic anchor and discrete index marks. The composition does not depend on filled backgrounds.

Reading hierarchy

Long-form headline first; then a shared index, consistent stage starts and a separate descriptions column. No repeated card edges.

Reflow

All phases remain in the same order. On mobile, descriptions move beneath each title, avoiding squeezed columns or horizontal scrolling.

DESIGN REVIEW / ACCEPTANCE

Review the composition.
Not the number of colors.

Visual identity

With the labels hidden, could either layout still feel meaningfully connected to Geniotek, rather than a generic five-step SaaS template?

Hierarchy

Can a reader understand the overall service and the five stages in sequence without confusing the illustration with a live project status?

Spatial consistency

Do text starts, row boundaries and color territories behave as a coherent system, including under longer copy and at mobile widths?

Contrast & scope

All colors here are solid, no photography or gradients. The violet field in A is a brand moment, not a default component background; neither variant defines new approved tokens.

Content is illustrative. The five actual phase names are shared; descriptions are working copy for design evaluation. This study does not modify the accepted Typography, Grid or existing Color Study.
