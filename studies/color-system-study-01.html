03 / Methodology

The method should work
without opening a link.

These are designer/agent-facing rules embedded in the study. Original source approaches are distinguished from Geniotek's proposed use.

IBM / Infrastructure

Define relationships before color values.

Documented IBM Carbon method. Neutral gray surfaces organize content into layered zones. Base, layer 01, layer 02, and layer 03 each introduce a context for text, borders, fields, and interaction states. The role represented by a token does not change from light to dark; the token's color value does. Core token groups include backgrounds, layers, fields, borders, text, links, icons, focus, and support states. An action color is not interchangeable with support-error or support-warning just because both draw attention.

Implementation choice for Geniotek. Use a compact vocabulary of semantic roles, with no fixed number of gradients or accents imposed on page art direction. Keep content-heavy pages mostly neutral, and protect important actions with verified foreground/background combinations. Technical status always includes a text label or symbol in addition to its hue.

Agent check. For every color use, state the role: brand identity, foreground text, interactive control, neutral surface, status, data differentiation, or expressive art direction. If a single color is performing conflicting jobs, split the roles even if they temporarily share the same HEX.

ABCD / Chromatic identity

Make the composition express the idea.

Documented ABCD method. In Canban, the concept of “half” informed a two-part graphic system, and color differentiation helped identify product attributes. This is not evidence of a universal two-color rule. In EUC, the studio's discussion of proportion, grids, and restrained design argues for order emerging from the subject matter; it does not publish Geniotek-ready color ratios.

Original proposal for Geniotek. “Visible / Invisible” is a hypothesis to test on product sourcing, engineering investigation, and factory audit communication. Violet can support the known product intention, while a charcoal or neutral region can support investigation and verification. The exact hue–meaning mapping is still provisional. In other contexts, a technical document may need no violet surface at all.

Agent check. Before creating a colored zone, write one sentence saying what information relationship it expresses. Then turn off the color and ask whether hierarchy and reading order still make sense. Avoid making every page a compulsory purple/black split. When expressive color is removed, the fundamental content must remain coherent.

Geniotek / Decision framework

One brand; adjustable intensity.

Low expression: engineering reports, support tables, legal and process documentation. Prioritize near-neutral surfaces, precise status markers and strong text contrast.

Medium expression: service explanations, landing page modules, case narratives. Preserve a calm structure and use brand violet as a selective anchor or orientation mark.

High expression: major hero statements, campaign pages and carefully chosen brand moments. Allow more saturated fields, division or transitions when the brand concept genuinely calls for them.

Non-negotiables: check the actual text/background pairing; ensure interactive states are recognizable; do not present color as the only status cue; distinguish real engineering results from fictional demonstration values. Color, typography, grid, signs and motion remain cooperating systems—not a hierarchy where one forbids the other.

Agent workflow / Before outputting Color CSS

1. Identify the information task and content density. 2. Select low, medium, or high chromatic intensity with a rationale. 3. Assign semantic roles. 4. Select provisional values and check contrast. 5. Test in representative page compositions, including longer copy and different widths. 6. State the unverified choices rather than claiming they are approved tokens.
